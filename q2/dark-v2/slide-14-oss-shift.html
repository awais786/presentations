How Open Source Is Changing
The ecosystem is moving away from PR counting - and our strategy moved first
01
The Signal: Hacktoberfest Drops PR Counts
Hacktoberfest 2026 has abandoned the pull-request-count model after a decade. AI made low-effort PRs trivial to generate, drowning maintainers. The event is now community sessions built around hands-on building. Our October plan follows suit: in-house hackathon sessions instead of PR-count drives.
"This year, we're not counting PRs." - DigitalOcean, Hacktoberfest 2026
hacktoberfest.com
02
The Evidence: Review Is the Bottleneck
AI agents opened 17M pull requests a month on GitHub by March 2026, up from 4M in September 2025 - and maintainers report roughly 1 in 10 is worth reviewing. Code got cheap; review did not. The question moved from "which issue can we fix" to "where can we create the most value".
curl ended its six-year bug bounty in Jan 2026 as valid reports fell from over 15% to under 5%. Jazzband - 84 Python projects, 3,135 members - sunset in Mar 2026, citing the same flood.
GitHub · curl · jazzband.co · Sep 2025 - Mar 2026
03
Our Response: Build and Own
Focus shifted from contributing into projects managed by someone else to building open source tools the company owns: the MCP servers, mpass-auth-proxy, the SSO layer, the Open edX health dashboard. Real requirements from tools we run ourselves, shipped publicly - broader open source exposure with durable ownership.
Why It Matters Owned projects compound: every improvement lands in our repos, under our name, reviewable at our pace - instead of waiting in someone else's queue.
